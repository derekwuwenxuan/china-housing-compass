# Parcel history, environmental legacy and cultural acceptance

Investigate historical land use at explicit geographic scopes. Keep verified physical hazards separate from cultural acceptance, personal comfort and resale-liquidity sensitivity.

## Contents

7. Translate findings into decisions

## 1. Separate geographic scopes

Research and report each scope independently:

1. `exact_parcel` — the legally identified parcel boundary;
2. `within_500m` — nearby uses and pathways approximately 500 metres from the boundary;
3. `within_1km` — wider nuisance, infrastructure and historical context;
4. `submarket` or `district` — broader area history and research leads.

Record the source geometry, date range, distance and direction when available. Do not assign a broader-area use to the exact parcel without boundary-level evidence.

Treat any reported farm, burial-ground, industrial or chemical history as a geographically scoped research lead. Never state that an exact parcel had that use unless cadastral, map, permit, environmental or equivalent evidence establishes the match.

## 2. Follow the source order

Prefer sources in this order:

1. cadastral, land-transfer, planning and parcel-boundary records;
2. environmental-impact documents, official contaminated-land lists and regulatory disclosures;
3. remediation plans, completion reports, acceptance records and land-use restrictions;
4. industrial-enterprise registrations, permits, government gazetteers and official archives;
5. historical maps, aerial imagery and official flood, drainage, groundwater or subsidence records;
6. professional media, local histories and attributable technical analysis;
7. field observations, social recollections and anonymous claims as supporting leads only.

Match the parcel number, coordinates, boundary, historical name and active dates. Record uncertainty when maps use different boundaries or georeferencing is approximate.

## 3. Assign a finding state

Assign every historical or environmental claim exactly one state:

- `officially_verified` — an inspectable primary record establishes the scoped claim;
- `multi_source_supported` — independent attributable sources converge without a decisive primary record;
- `lead_only` — a source identifies a question but does not establish the scoped fact;
- `unverified_rumor` — the claim is unattributed, contradicted or not reproducible;
- `unknown` — available evidence cannot determine the history or condition.

Also record geographic scope, active dates, distance and direction, source grade, current use, remediation status, residual uncertainty and the evidence needed to upgrade the state. Preserve conflicting claims with their separate scopes; do not select the more alarming version without evidence.

## 4. Investigate historical uses without automatic stigma

For former agricultural land, do not presume harm. Check fill origin and compaction, pesticide or fertilizer legacy, drainage, groundwater and settlement only when documents or site conditions warrant the question.

For reported burial grounds, verify exact boundaries and dates through cadastral, planning, historical-map or official local-history evidence. Keep any soil, archaeological or construction implication in the physical assessment. Keep buyer discomfort, taboo and resale perception in the cultural assessment. Do not assert supernatural harm.

Without exact-parcel evidence and an evidenced physical mechanism, any burial-ground statement, area history, recollection or cultural concern has a default valuation weight of zero. It must not enter a price adjustment, contamination finding, physical-risk conclusion or recommendation. Preserve it only as a geographically scoped research lead or, when the buyer says it matters personally, a cultural-acceptance/resale-liquidity sensitivity. Nearby or submarket evidence never upgrades itself to exact-parcel evidence.

For industrial, chemical, landfill, backfill, mining, workshop, fuel-storage or wastewater history, identify the operator, process, dates, footprint, likely pollutants and migration pathways. Search soil and groundwater testing, contaminated-land status, remediation scope, acceptance, residual controls and sensitive residential-use suitability.

For water bodies, floodplain or filled ponds, investigate drainage, flood/waterlogging, fill quality, settlement, groundwater and foundation evidence. Do not turn an old map symbol into a current defect without verification.

## 5. Verify contamination and remediation

Use this workflow when industrial, chemical, landfill or contamination evidence appears:

1. Confirm whether the source concerns the exact parcel, a nearby parcel or only the broader area.
2. Identify the historical operator, process, active period and candidate contaminants.
3. Inspect official investigations, risk assessments, contaminated-land listings and environmental-impact documents.
4. Inspect the remediation boundary, method, target standards and completion evidence.
5. Verify regulatory acceptance, current land-use restrictions and suitability for sensitive residential use.
6. Record residual groundwater, vapor, off-site migration, monitoring and uncertainty.
7. State the valuation treatment and whether the evidence creates a veto, reserve, verification condition or no demonstrated adjustment.

Do not equate “remediation completed” with zero residual risk. Do not equate area-wide chemical history with exact-parcel contamination. Use `unknown` when exact-parcel evidence is absent.

## 6. Separate physical risk from cultural acceptance

Create two assessments.

### Objective physical environment

Assess evidence for contamination, odor, noise, vibration, traffic safety, rail or high-voltage proximity, sunlight, ventilation, privacy, flood/waterlogging, slope, subsidence, backfill and incompatible nearby uses. Explain the mechanism and evidence before applying a veto, reserve or comparable adjustment.

### Cultural or feng-shui acceptance

Record buyer sensitivity as `ignore`, `standard` or `high`. Review observable conditions associated in Chinese purchasing culture with road alignment, curved roads, narrow building gaps, sharp structures, elevated roads, burial grounds, temples, hospitals, funeral facilities, water and topography.

Do not assert objective good or bad luck, supernatural causation or a universal monetary discount. Translate the observable feature into cultural acceptance, personal comfort, negotiation friction, target-buyer narrowing or resale-liquidity sensitivity. Keep objective counterparts such as noise, safety, sunlight, wind and privacy in the physical assessment.

## 7. Translate findings into the decision

- Treat unresolved official contamination, failed remediation or unsuitable sensitive-use evidence as a potential veto.
- Treat verified odor, noise, drainage, access, neighboring-use or persistent market-stigma evidence as a possible comparable adjustment or risk reserve.
- Treat area-level history, recollection and rumor as research prompts with no automatic valuation weight.
- Treat cultural factors as user-fit and liquidity considerations calibrated to the user's sensitivity, not as scientific hazards.
- Show each applied treatment with its evidence, scope, confidence and mechanism.

State what remains unknown and what inspection, map, record or test would change the recommendation.
