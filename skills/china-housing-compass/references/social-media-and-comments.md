# Social-media and comment evidence

Use public social evidence to discover risks, verification questions and marketability concerns. Do not use sentiment alone to prove a transaction, violation, defect, environmental condition or price effect.

## Contents

1. Access ladder
2. Search matrix and coverage
3. Capture and classify evidence
4. Sample comments and disagreement
5. Grade and verify claims
6. Handle blocked or weak sources
7. Protect privacy and copyright

## 1. Follow the access ladder

Use this order for each platform:

1. Search and inspect content available without authentication.
2. Treat an indexed snippet as a lead when the underlying post or comment cannot be inspected.
3. Ask the user to authorize access to an already logged-in browser only when decisive visible public content requires a normal login and that capability is available.
   When the user specifically asks for comment consensus or quotations and the underlying thread requires a normal login, make this authorization request explicit rather than only reporting the access gap.
4. Read only content the user can normally view through that authorized browser.
5. Record the failed source and missing sample when access or the thread remains unavailable.

Record one access mode: `public_web`, `indexed_snippet`, `authorized_browser`, `user_supplied`, or `unavailable`.

Never request a password, cookie, token or other credential. Never automate account login, bypass CAPTCHA or anti-bot controls, defeat a platform restriction, access private content, or assume an authorized-browser capability exists. Continue with a stated limitation when access is unavailable.

## 2. Search the evidence matrix

Combine project and entity terms:

- formal project name, marketing aliases, historical name, parcel number and address;
- city, district, street and submarket;
- legal project company, developer brand, contractor, supervisor and property manager.

Combine them with issue terms covering:

- delivery, construction progress, quality, fit-out and reduction in specification;
- price cuts, inventory, resale, rent and rights protection;
- noise, odor, waterlogging, school, transport and nearby uses;
- model home, garden, terrace, property service and resident experience.

Search Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Weibo, Zhihu, Bilibili, local forums and other relevant platforms. For full due diligence, target at least two available platforms, ten independent posts and thirty visible comments. Treat these as coverage targets, not proof of representativeness. Report actual counts, unavailable platforms, dates and every shortfall.

## 3. Capture and classify each item

Record, when observable:

- platform, URL or stable locator, query and access mode;
- published, observed and retrieved dates;
- post/comment relationship and visible engagement;
- author role: developer, intermediary, owner, tenant, buyer or visitor, local resident, media, or unknown;
- content type: advertisement, experience, complaint, question, news, analysis, or repost;
- commercial interest and possible coordinated or copied-content markers;
- stance: positive, negative, mixed, neutral, or unknown;
- themes, concise paraphrase, source grade and caveat;
- factual allegations that need primary-source verification.

Classify an author only from observable evidence. Use `unknown` instead of inferring ownership, employment, demographics or motive. Deduplicate reposts, copied marketing language and the same content distributed across accounts.

## 4. Sample comments and preserve disagreement

Select recent and high-engagement items while deliberately including positive, negative, mixed and dissenting views. Sample across authors, dates, threads and platforms rather than taking the first visible comments. Keep replies linked to their parent item and distinguish post claims from comment claims.

Before synthesizing themes, show:

- platforms and time window;
- independent post count and visible comment count;
- requested count versus obtained count;
- commercial or coordinated-content share when observable;
- inaccessible, deleted or marketing-dominated sources.

Describe each recurring theme with its captured basis and counterexamples. Preserve material disagreement, minority views and uncertainty. Do not suppress favorable evidence because a complaint is vivid, or suppress complaints because marketing content dominates the sample.

Do not invent a theme, quote, count, engagement measure, author identity, stance proportion, sentiment score or consensus. Do not describe “owner consensus,” “platform consensus” or a representative public view when identity, coverage, independence or time diversity is insufficient. If the user requests a sentiment score, label it as a measure of the documented captured sample only and show the denominator and method.

## 5. Grade and verify claims

- Grade an inspectable, attributable public post or comment C or D according to identity and corroboration; never grade ordinary sentiment A.
- Grade an indexed snippet with no inspectable underlying item D and use it only as a lead.
- Treat developer or intermediary content as commercially interested even when factually useful.
- Verify material claims about permits, law, contamination, construction defects, delivery, price cuts, escrow or penalties through primary records.
- Keep a social allegation visibly separate from any official verification and from valuation treatment.

Use social evidence to prioritize research and assess marketability. Apply a price adjustment or risk reserve only when the underlying mechanism and evidence support it independently.

## 6. Handle blocked, deleted and weak sources

For each failed attempt, record the platform, query or locator, attempted access mode, retrieval time, failure reason and missing coverage. Preserve prior metadata when content later disappears, mark it stale or unavailable, and do not treat deletion as proof of the claim.

When comments cannot be inspected, state **zero captured comments**. Omit comment quotations, theme claims, stance percentages and consensus language. You may report an indexed snippet only as a D-grade lead and clearly state that the underlying item was not inspected.

When the sample is small, one-sided, time-concentrated or dominated by marketing, lower confidence and state the bias. Seek independent owners, residents, buyers, visitors and primary records rather than filling gaps with inference.

## 7. Protect privacy and copyright

Paraphrase by default. Retain only a short excerpt when auditability requires it and source rules permit it. Do not reproduce complete posts, images, videos or comment threads.

Exclude unnecessary usernames, avatars, phone numbers, exact location trails and personal identifiers from reports and public fixtures. Keep raw captures, usernames and authorized-browser state private and outside the public repository. Publish only synthetic fixtures, minimized permitted excerpts and aggregate examples with provenance.
