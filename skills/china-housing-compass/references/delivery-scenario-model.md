# Delivery scenario model

Presale value must be assessed at delivery and discounted back to today.

```text
delivery value = current comparable value
                 × city factor
                 × submarket factor
                 × project factor
                 × product factor

maximum purchase price today = delivery net realizable value / (1 + required return)^T
                               - purchase costs
                               - financing/opportunity costs
                               - risk reserve

delivery loss rate = (buyer all-in cost - delivery net realizable value)
                     / buyer all-in cost
```

`T` is fractional years to contractual or revised delivery. State whether delivery net realizable value already deducts resale/friction/repair costs.

## Mandatory conditional rows

1. **Upside:** supportive city/submarket, on-time delivery, product and facilities meet evidenced expectations.
2. **Base:** conservative central assumptions, no unverified promised-facility value.
3. **Downside:** weaker prices/liquidity, project discounting and partial product penalty.
4. **Severe liquidity stress:** forced-sale discount, abundant competing stock and material product/project penalty.

Add **delay** and **quality dispute** when evidence warrants. Do not attach probabilities unless a defensible statistical base exists.

## Factor discipline

Explain every factor and evidence. Avoid double-counting: if the current comparable already reflects submarket conditions, a separate submarket factor should represent only forecast change to delivery. Do not apply the same construction concern in project factor, product factor and risk reserve without showing the separation.

Consider city/submarket momentum, population/income, supply/absorption, policy and mortgage conditions; then project discounting, official inventory, construction/delivery, product quality, infrastructure realization and resale competition. Never mechanically annualize one monthly change.

Output values as ranges when factor evidence is weak. The lowest stress result is a conditional scenario, not “the bottom.”

## China-first one-to-three-year scenario path

For every presale that delivers within or near the next three years, show year 1, year 2 and year 3 when the horizon is decision-relevant. Use at least **base**, **weak** and **stress** paths; keep the existing upside and severe-liquidity-stress rows when warranted. State the forecast as of date, evidence window and which year contains contractual/revised delivery.

Give greatest weight to implemented national/local policy, the economic and employment outlook, rates/credit, population and household formation, income/affordability and leverage, youth consumption/tenure preferences, new-home and resale supply, completed/unsold stock, absorption, land, local-government finance, developer funding/delivery capacity and infrastructure realization. Separate national, city, submarket, project and product effects and avoid double-counting.

Kondratiev and other long-wave cycle narratives may appear only as low-weight context. A user-supplied price or market-direction forecast is a dated, attributed, case-scoped, zero-weight hypothesis by default and may be shown in a requested stress path. Neither can set a universal decline rate, probability or price floor.
